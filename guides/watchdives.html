Watchdives watches review: are they any good?

The three Watchdives rows we track are priced from watchdives.com, the brand’s own store, and each carries the date it was last checked; fidelity follows the published rubric. Short answer: yes — and the first thing to understand is that Watchdives is a shop as well as a brand. We read its entire catalogue on 20 September 2026: 261 listings, of which 120 carry Watchdives as the vendor and only 65 are Watchdives’ own watches. The rest of the store is straps and cases, plus ten other houses it simply retails. That single fact explains most of the confusion around the name, and it is where this review starts.

Watchdives is a shop as well as a brand

Search the name and you will land on a storefront that also sells San Martin (35 listings), Thorn (23), Addiesdive (18), Cronos (16), Militado (14), Octopus Kraken, Tactical Frog, Rdunae, Chameri and Hruodland. None of those is a Watchdives watch. If you are comparing “a Watchdives” against a San Martin and the listing you are reading came off watchdives.com, check the vendor line first — there is a real chance you are comparing San Martin with San Martin.

This is not a criticism. A factory-direct store that also carries its neighbours is how most of this segment reaches the West, and it is why Watchdives has one of the deeper strap and bracelet catalogues in the field. But it does mean the brand’s own line is smaller than the store makes it look: 65 watches, not 261.

What Watchdives gets right

Titanium, vintage divers, and naming the reference it follows. Watchdives is one of the few houses at this money putting grade-5 titanium cases into a sub-$200 watch — the EXD-40 is $129 in natural titanium — and its vintage-Submariner and Fifty Fathoms readings run on Swiss-class PT5000 and SL3001 automatics rather than the Seiko NH35 that the budget tier defaults to.

It also names its references after the originals, which is rarer than it sounds: WD16570 is the Explorer II reference, WD5512 and WD5513 are Submariner references, WD6263 is a Daytona reference and WD1965 points at the 1965 Seiko 62MAS. That first-party attribution is why three Watchdives rows carry a verified date here rather than a guess — we are not inferring the target from a photograph.

Stock is healthier than the sold-out headlines suggest: 57 of the 65 own-brand watches had at least one variant available at our 20 September read.

Where it falls short

The quartz share, and the sold-out traps. 38 of the 65 watches are quartz against 24 automatic — the same split Addiesdive has, and the same warning applies: the Seiko VH31 sweeps rather than ticks, so it reads mechanical at arm’s length and costs roughly half, but the listing will not shout about it. VH31 is named in 26 of the store’s own titles, more than any other calibre.

The bigger practical problem is that the model people most want is often the one that is gone. The WD16570 V2, the highest-scoring Watchdives watch we track at fidelity 81, had all five variants sold out on 20 September — and it had all five sold out when we checked on 4 September, and again on 18 August. A reference can sit listed and unbuyable here for months, so treat any “in stock” claim about it from a third-party listicle with suspicion.

Fidelity across our three Watchdives rows runs 71 to 81 on the rubric, against San Martin’s mid-80s.

Where are Watchdives watches made?

China, sold factory-direct. Unlike most of the field, Watchdives runs its own affiliate programme alongside its Amazon presence, which is why several rows on this site link the maker’s own product page rather than a marketplace search. Where both sellers pass our checks, the cheaper one is shown first — never the one that pays more.

Watchdives vs Addiesdive vs San Martin

Three price tiers, cleanly separated. Addiesdive is the $79–$150 spec-per-dollar play and owns the correct 36mm Explorer. San Martin sits in the $280–$465 range with the best finishing in the segment. Watchdives lands between them at a $74–$459 band, median $149, and wins on two things neither answers cheaply: titanium cases, and vintage dive references on Swiss-class automatics. If you want the best-finished homage, San Martin. The cheapest credible one, Addiesdive. A titanium diver or a 1960s Submariner reading for under $200, Watchdives.

The model numbers people search, and what they actually are

Watchdives names everything by reference, so most searches for the brand are searches for a code — and several of the most-searched ones return more than one product. From its own catalogue, read 20 September 2026:

  • WD1863 — the VK63 chronograph, $129–$139 across a base listing and Classic and Panda collections. Worth flagging because the store also sells a “WD1863 Chronograph Watch Case” at $65, which is a case, not a watch, and was out of stock at our read. If a $65 WD1863 turns up in a search result, that is what it is.
  • Watchdives Premier — two different things. The WDC0001 Premier is a $459 Miyota 9015 automatic sold under the separate WDC sub-brand; the Watchdives Premier WD0019 is a $289 ST2130 diver. Same word, a $170 gap, different product lines.
  • EXD — two sizes, and only one buyable. The EXD-40 is $129 natural titanium or $139 black, 3 of 6 variants in stock; the EXD-38 is $159 and was sold out across all 4 variants.
  • WD7922 — the 37mm VH31 quartz the store’s own handle calls a BB54, $119, fully in stock.
  • WD1972 — the VH31 military diver V2 at $85. Listed, but all 3 variants were sold out on 20 September.
  • WD16570 vs WD16570B — the single most useful distinction on this page. The $229 WD16570 V2 NH34 automatic is sold out in all five variants; the $189 WD16570B on the 6460 GMT is a different watch and was partly in stock. They are not variants of each other.
  • WD1967 Sharkmaster 300 — a $95 automatic, sold out at our read.
  • WD6263 — a $179 vintage racing chronograph, 8 of 8 in stock. The 6263 is a Daytona reference, but Watchdives does not name the original in the listing and we do not score a row we would have to infer, so it carries no fidelity number here.

What Watchdives does not make

The store is broad enough that it is easy to credit the brand with watches it never built. Across the 65 own-brand watches read on 20 September, Watchdives’ own titles point at dive and GMT references almost exclusively — Submariner, Fifty Fathoms, 62MAS, Explorer II, Black Bay — plus chronographs. There is no Watchdives Nautilus, Royal Oak, Datejust, Day-Date, Santos or Tank. Where a listicle credits one, it is almost always a watch the store retails from another house rather than one Watchdives makes.

Bottom line: Watchdives is the titanium-and-vintage-diver corner of the affordable homage world, $74–$459 with a median of $149, and the only house here running Swiss-class automatics into sub-$200 Submariner and Fifty Fathoms readings. Two things to check before you buy: whether the listing is actually a Watchdives watch rather than one of the ten houses it retails, and whether the reference you want is one of the 38 quartz models or the 24 automatics.
