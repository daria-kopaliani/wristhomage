Are San Martin watches any good?

10 of the 14 San Martin rows we track are priced from sanmartinwatches.com, the brand’s own store, and each row carries the date it was last checked; fidelity follows the published rubric. Short answer: yes. Ask any homage forum "what is the step up from Pagani Design?" and San Martin is the answer you will get. Our database tracks fourteen San Martin models across a $218–$460 band, and they hold the top mid-tier spot on most icons they cover. Here is what the brand actually gets right, where it falls short, and the right San Martin for each design.

What San Martin gets right

Finishing, mainly — the part budget homages skip. Cases get real brushing with crisp transitions, bezels turn without slop, and the bracelets are good enough that nobody swaps them, which is the first thing owners do at the tier below. Sapphire is standard across the range. Movements are proven and serviceable: the Seiko NH35 workhorse on most divers, the Swiss-workalike PT5000 (an ETA 2824 clone-class movement) as a frequent option, the smoother Miyota 9015 in the dressier pieces, and Seiko's NH34 where a GMT hand is needed. Just as important, quality control is consistent — you are not playing the alignment lottery that budget brands make you play.

Where it falls short

Price, at the top of the range. Approaching $390 a San Martin is close to used-Swiss and microbrand money, and brands like Steinhart sit only a step above with genuinely Swiss movements. These are also homages, not original designs — you are buying someone else's design language executed well, which matters to some people and not at all to others. And resale is thin; buy one to wear it, not to trade it. One honest buying note: most models are carried on Amazon, but San Martin sells factory-direct too, and direct is often a little cheaper for the patient.

Where are San Martin watches made?

China. San Martin is based in Guangzhou and runs a factory-direct model, which is most of the explanation for the finishing-per-dollar. There is no Swiss theatre here and the brand does not pretend otherwise — the value case rests entirely on what the machining and assembly deliver for the money.

San Martin vs Pagani Design

The comparison everyone actually wants. Pagani Design is maximum look for minimum spend: mostly under $130, genuine automatics, designs that read instantly — with bracelets and QC as the accepted weak points. San Martin charges two to three times more and puts the difference where your fingers land: bracelets, bezel action, dial printing, lume, consistency. If you want to try a design cheaply, start with Pagani. If you already know you will notice the details, skip straight to the San Martin and buy once.

Homages San Martin does not actually make

Worth knowing before you go looking, because the internet will tell you otherwise. We crawled all 319 products on San Martin's own store in August 2026 and found no Fifty Fathoms homage, no Portugieser, no Milgauss and no Panerai-style cushion watch — and no SN011 or SN041 reference at all, despite both appearing in listicles. We had all four filed here ourselves until that check. The 39mm "Leyenda" super-compressor (SN0141W) is real but is a Watchdives collaboration sold through them, not by San Martin.

One more caution: model numbers in this brand are unforgiving. SN004-G is a 38mm Submariner-style diver with no GMT hand, while the GMT-Master homage is the SN005 family — a single digit apart, two different watches.

Bottom line: San Martin is the tier where homages stop feeling like compromises. Pay roughly double Pagani money, get finishing that embarrasses watches at triple the price, and pick your model by the icon you actually want on your wrist.
