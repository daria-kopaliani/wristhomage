The best Daytona homage

Last updated August 2026.

A chronograph is the one complication that genuinely costs money to build, so the affordable Daytona field is defined by a compromise the dive-watch field never has to make: almost none of these are automatic. Understand what meca-quartz is and the whole category makes sense — and mostly in your favour. Here are the three worth knowing, ranked on our fidelity rubric.

Meca-quartz, and why it is not a cop-out

An automatic chronograph movement is expensive — it is the single most complex thing in a mainstream mechanical watch, and putting one in a $150 homage is not possible. Meca-quartz splits the difference: a quartz movement keeps time, while a genuine mechanical module drives the chronograph. In practice that means the sweep is smooth rather than ticking, the pushers have real mechanical resistance, and the reset hand snaps back to zero the way an expensive chronograph does. What you lose is the automatic winding and the story. Two of the three watches below are meca-quartz; the third is a hand-wound mechanical, and it costs what that implies.

1. Pagani Design PD-1664 — the value benchmark, and the right first buy Amazon ↗

About $105, fidelity 82. The panda dial and tachymeter bezel that make the Daytona what it is, driven by a Seiko VK63 meca-quartz — the seconds hand sweeps, the pushers feel mechanical, and the whole thing costs about the same as a nice dinner. 40mm and 100m water resistance, matching the original's case size exactly. This is where almost everyone should start, and Pagani Design built its reputation on precisely this watch.

2. San Martin SN0116 — the cleaner build Amazon ↗

About $360, fidelity 83. The same meca-quartz idea executed with mid-tier care: a Seiko VK64 module, a ceramic chronograph bezel, cleaner dial printing and a noticeably better bracelet than the budget tier. It runs 39.5mm against the original's 40mm. It scores a point higher on fidelity and feels several tiers better in the hand — but be clear about what the extra $255 buys, because it is not a mechanical movement. It is finishing, printing and bracelet. If those are what bother you about the Pagani, this fixes them; if they are not, save the money. Usually cheaper direct from San Martin.

3. Sugess Panda Chrono — the mechanical purist's pick

About $220, fidelity 78. The one genuinely mechanical option: a hand-wound Seagull ST1901, a column-wheel-style Chinese chronograph with real history behind it. You wind it every day, the chronograph is driven by gears rather than a module, and the watch has a character neither meca-quartz option can imitate. The compromises are honest ones — the lowest fidelity of the three, and only 50m water resistance, so treat it as a dress chronograph. Usually sourced direct rather than through Amazon.

The honest summary: buy the Pagani at $105 unless you have a specific reason not to — it matches the original's 40mm case, and meca-quartz is the right answer at this price. Step to the San Martin only if dial printing and bracelet quality are what you notice. Choose the Sugess only if "mechanical" is non-negotiable, and accept a softer homage and a watch you have to wind. Compare the full field on the Daytona homages page.
