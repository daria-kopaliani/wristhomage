The best Omega Speedmaster homage

This page used to rank three watches. After checking every one against the maker's own catalogue in August 2026, one of them turned out not to exist, one turned out to be a homage to a completely different watch, and the third is a homage to a different Speedmaster than the one you are probably picturing. What follows is the honest field, and the two-second test that tells you whether a "Speedmaster homage" is one at all.

Count the sub-dials

The Moonwatch is a tricompax chronograph: three registers, at three, six and nine o'clock, under a tachymeter bezel. Most affordable "Speedmaster homages" are bicompax — two sub-dials, at three and nine, usually with no tachymeter bezel at all. Bicompax vintage chronographs are a whole genre with their own ancestry, and they are not this watch. Count the sub-dials before you read the listing copy and most of the confusion in this category disappears.

A second cue trips up even careful buyers, including us: straight lugs and no crown guards describe the 1957 Speedmaster, not the Moonwatch Professional, which has an asymmetric case with crown guards. Judging by that cue pulls '57-style watches into a Moonwatch shortlist where they do not belong.

1. Pagani Design PD-1701 — the only one that genuinely qualifies

About $136, fidelity 80. The stepped dial, the three registers and a moulded tachymeter bezel, on a Seiko VK63 meca-quartz. What settles the attribution is that Pagani Design says so itself: its own catalogue files the PD-1701 as a nod to the Omega Speedmaster, in the same breath as filing the PD-1664 under Daytona. It runs 40mm against the original's 42mm and carries 100m of water resistance to the Moonwatch's 50m.

The honest limit is the movement. Meca-quartz means a quartz module keeps time while a mechanical module drives the chronograph — the pushers feel right and the seconds hand snaps back, but there is no winding ritual and nothing to look at through a caseback. For most people at this price that is the correct trade; if it is not, the next entry is the alternative, with a caveat.

2. San Martin SN0103-G-JS — hand-wound, but a different Speedmaster

About $349, fidelity 72. This is the watch to know about if the winding ritual is the point: a genuine Seagull ST1901 hand-wound column-wheel chronograph, with the best case and bracelet work in this field by some distance. It is also the row we had most wrong — we listed it for months as a Seiko VK64 meca-quartz, which it is not.

Read the layout before you buy. San Martin's own name for it is the SM57, and it is bicompax with straight lugs and no tachymeter bezel: it follows the 1957 Speedmaster CK2915, the Moonwatch's ancestor, rather than the moon-worn Professional. Against the Moonwatch as a reference it scores below the cheaper Pagani for exactly that reason. As a watch it is the better object; as a Moonwatch homage it is the wrong decade. Usually cheaper direct from San Martin, and frequently out of stock.

What we removed, and why

Two entries came off this page in August 2026, and both are still recommended widely elsewhere.

The "Pagani Design PD-1963" does not exist. We ranked it here as the $110 value pick. Pagani's catalogue runs to 129 models and contains no PD-1963 — the number appears to have been borrowed from the Chinese 1963 chronograph and attached to the wrong brand. Anyone who followed our buy link was searching for a watch nobody makes.

The Sugess 1963 is not a Speedmaster homage. It is a reissue of the Chinese Air Force pilot chronograph developed by the Tianjin Watch Factory from 1961 and delivered to PLAAF pilots in the mid-sixties; the ST19 movement inside every modern "1963" descends directly from that project, and Sugess's own product copy describes exactly that lineage with no mention of Omega. It is bicompax, has no tachymeter bezel, and rates 50m. It is a genuinely good watch with real history — just somebody else's history. We have moved it out of this field rather than keep flattering it with the wrong comparison.

The honest summary: the affordable Moonwatch homage field is one watch deep. Buy the Pagani PD-1701 at $136 if you want the Moonwatch look and can live with meca-quartz. Buy the San Martin SM57 at $349 if you want a real hand-wound column-wheel chronograph and are happy that it follows the 1957 Speedmaster instead. If a list offers you a third option, count its sub-dials first. Compare the full field on the Speedmaster homages page.
