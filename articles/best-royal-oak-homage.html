The best AP Royal Oak homage

The Royal Oak is an integrated-bracelet watch, and that is the expensive part. The octagonal bezel with its eight exposed screws is easy enough to reproduce; what separates a good homage from a cheap one is whether the bracelet grows out of the case properly and whether the dial's tapisserie grid has any depth to it. Three watches in our database are worth your attention, ranked on our fidelity rubric.

Stamped versus machined: the detail that sets the tiers

On a real Royal Oak the tapisserie grid is machined into the dial, so every small square has depth and the dial changes character as light moves across it. Affordable homages stamp the pattern instead. Stamping gets the geometry right — the squares are the correct size and spacing — but the result is flatter and more uniform, and in certain light it reads as texture printed on rather than cut in. That single difference accounts for most of the gap between the three watches below, and it is the thing to look at in photographs before you buy.

1. San Martin SN013-G — the best overall

About $375, fidelity 83. The pick when the dial texture matters to you: a genuinely close tapisserie and noticeably better bracelet integration, running a Miyota 9015 automatic that is smoother than the Seiko movements in the budget tier. It is the smallest here at 38.5mm against the original's 41mm, and only 9.5mm thick — which is the point, because the Royal Oak is a thin watch and the budget tier is not. San Martin describes the bezel as a Royal Oak/Nautilus hybrid rather than a straight copy. 100m water resistance. Usually cheaper direct from San Martin.

2. Pagani Design PD-1673 — the value benchmark

About $158, fidelity 78. The octagonal bezel with exposed screws, a tapisserie-style dial and an integrated bracelet for well under half the San Martin's price. The pattern is stamped rather than machined and the score reflects it, but two things go the Pagani's way: it carries 100m water resistance — twice the San Martin's — and adds a sapphire crystal, which is rare at the price. A Seiko NH35 does the work inside. For most people wanting the integrated-bracelet look without a four-figure commitment, this is the sensible buy. See Pagani Design, model by model.

3. Cadisen C8180 — the cheapest real automatic

About $87, fidelity 75. The least money that still buys a genuine Seiko NH35A automatic in the octagonal-bezel silhouette, with an exhibition caseback so you can watch it work. Two honest caveats: it wears a full 42mm against the original's 41mm — the largest of the three here, and on a design this angular that is felt — and the waffle dial is stamped with less crispness than the Pagani's. In its favour, 100m of water resistance, double what the Royal Oak itself offers. If the Pagani's $158 is more than you wanted to spend, this is the trade you are making — and it is a $71 saving, not a rounding error.

If it is the Nautilus you actually want

The Royal Oak and the Patek Nautilus get confused constantly, and they are different watches — porthole case and grooved horizontal dial rather than octagon and grid. If the one in your head has rounded "ears" at the sides of the case, that is the Nautilus, and it has its own field led by the San Martin SN076-G and the Pagani PD-1728. See the Nautilus homages page.

The honest summary: the Pagani gets the water resistance and the crystal right for $158 and is the correct first integrated-bracelet watch. The Cadisen saves you $71 and costs you a millimetre of case and some dial crispness. Pay the San Martin premium for the bracelet integration and the 9.5mm case — it is the only one here that wears like the original rather than merely resembling it.
