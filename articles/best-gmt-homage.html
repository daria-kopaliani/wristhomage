The best Rolex GMT homage

Last updated August 2026.

For years the GMT was the one complication you could not get cheap — a fourth hand meant serious movement money. Then Seiko put the NH34 GMT movement on the open market and the affordable field got real overnight. Every watch here has a genuine, independently-set 24-hour hand; what separates them is what drives it. Here are the four worth owning, ranked on our fidelity rubric.

The movement part, first — and a correction

The received wisdom is that the whole budget GMT field runs the Seiko NH34. It does not, and we repeated that error here until August 2026. Checking each brand's own store turned up four different movements across these four watches: a Seiko NH34, a Chinese Pearl DG5833, a Swiss Sellita SW330 and a Swiss Ronda quartz. All four give you a true independently-set 24-hour hand, which is the thing that matters — but "it's the NH34" is a shortcut worth dropping, and one of them is not automatic at all.

The specific trap: the Pagani PD-1662 at around $122 is not an NH34 watch. Pagani sells an NH34 version of the same design as a separate model at about $320. If the movement is why you are buying, check which PD-1662 you are looking at.

1. San Martin SN0134-G1 — the best overall Amazon ↗

About $329, fidelity 87. The highest-fidelity watch in this field and the only one built at the original's exact 40mm. Tighter finishing than the budget set, 200m of water resistance, a red-and-blue sapphire bezel, and a genuine Seiko NH34 inside. It is the in-stock member of San Martin's SN005 GMT family — the canonical SN005-B-1S is the same watch for about $308 but is routinely sold out, which is the brand's chronic problem. Carried on Amazon, often a little cheaper direct from San Martin.

2. Pagani Design PD-1662 — the value benchmark Amazon ↗

About $122, fidelity 82. The homage that made Pagani Design famous: a true GMT hand, a ceramic Pepsi bezel and a jubilee bracelet at the original's 40mm for a fraction of everything else. The movement is a Pearl DG5833 rather than the Seiko it is usually credited with, and the bracelet and QC are where the price shows — but nothing else at this money gives you a real GMT complication. This is still where most people should start.

3. Steinhart Ocean One GMT BLUE-RED — the Swiss step-up

About $582, fidelity 86. The one watch here with a Swiss movement — a Sellita SW330-2 Elaboré — backed by dive-grade 300m water resistance in a 42mm case. Two things to know before you budget: that price excludes German VAT, which Steinhart deducts for buyers outside the EU (it is about $693 including it, before shipping and import duty), and "Ocean One GMT" is a family of eight variants, so pick the BLUE-RED if the Pepsi look is the point. No affiliate program and no Amazon presence — Steinhart sells direct, and we link it honestly.

4. Addiesdive AD2050 — the cheapest real GMT Amazon ↗

About $79, fidelity 68. The least money that still buys a genuine independently-set 24-hour hand, at the original's 40mm with a 200m rating. The honest catch, and the reason it scores where it does: it is a Swiss Ronda quartz, not an automatic. Addiesdive makes no NH34 automatic GMT at all — we listed one for months, and it does not exist. As a travel beater the quartz is arguably the more useful watch; as an automatic GMT it is not in the running. The Pepsi colourway was sold out on Addiesdive's own store at our last check.

Prefer the Explorer II look?

If your GMT itch is the fixed-bezel, orange-hand tool watch rather than the Pepsi, that is a different original — the Explorer II — with its own field: the titanium Timex Expedition Pioneer GMT at about $629, Baltany's S6073AB at about $283, and Pagani's PD-1693 at about $148 — the freccione orange hand for the least money. Those three do all run the same NH34. See the full ranked field on the Explorer II homages page.

The honest summary: most people should buy the Pagani and see if the GMT life sticks; enthusiasts should skip straight to the San Martin; Swiss-movement holdouts pay double for the Steinhart and know why. Compare the full field with prices and movements on the GMT-Master II homages page.
