The trap: two completely different waves

Search for a Seamaster homage and you will quickly meet the Steeldive SD1970, frequently listed as a "wave diver". It is not a Seamaster homage. It is a homage to the Seiko 6105 — the cushion-cased diver Martin Sheen wears in Apocalypse Now, which is why enthusiasts call it the Captain Willard. It is 43.8mm, 200m, and a genuinely well-regarded watch.

The confusion comes from one of its most popular dials: Hokusai's Great Wave off Kanagawa, printed as artwork. That is a picture of a wave. The Seamaster's dial is a texture — a repeating moulded wave pattern across the whole dial, not an image. Two watches, two unrelated waves, one word doing a lot of damage. If you want a 6105 homage the SD1970 is a good buy; it simply is not this.

We had this wrong ourselves until August 2026, when a catalogue audit caught it. It is corrected here and on the database page.

The one that genuinely is: Pagani Design PD-1685 Amazon ↗

About $190, fidelity 80. A real wave-textured dial, a ceramic bezel, curved sapphire and a Seiko NH35A automatic — built at 42mm, which is the modern Diver 300M's case size exactly, with 200m of water resistance against the original's 300m. The bracelet even reproduces the zig-zag link pattern the real one is known for.

At this price the compromises are where you would expect: the dial texture is moulded rather than laser-cut, the ceramic bezel insert is less crisply printed, and the case finishing is budget-tier. None of that stops it reading correctly on the wrist, which is the whole job. See Pagani Design, model by model.

If you meant the vintage Seamaster 300

"Seamaster" covers two very different watches. If the one in your head is the 1957-style vintage diver — domed crystal, no wave dial, faded-lume aesthetic — that is the Seamaster 300 heritage line, and we no longer publish a page for it. An August 2026 audit found that neither watch we listed there survived checking: San Martin has no SN011 reference in its 319-product catalogue, and the Steeldive "1957" is not a Heritage homage at all. That page is retired rather than filled with guesses.

The second one that genuinely is: Steeldive SD1957 Amazon ↗

About $128, fidelity 78. Do not be misled by the number. Steeldive names models after the year the original launched — SD1953 for the Submariner, SD1952 for the Fifty Fathoms — so the SD1957 reads like a Seamaster 300 Heritage homage. It is not: at 42mm with a wave dial and a 300m rating it follows the modern Diver 300M, the same watch the Pagani above does, and it is the only homage in our database matching the original's full 300m depth rating. Sapphire, Seiko auto, and the cheapest genuine entry into this design. We had this one filed under the wrong original until August 2026, seeded from a reseller site that also misstates its water resistance as 200m — the brand's own page says 300m.

The honest summary: if you want the modern Diver 300M look, the PD-1685 is the one in our database that actually is that watch, and at $190 it is still comfortably the cheapest honest route to that dial. If a listing calls something a "wave diver", check whether the wave is a texture or a picture before you buy — that single question separates a Seamaster homage from a Seiko one.
